What is this course about?

Modern machine learning is no longer dominated by a single generative paradigm. In addition to autoregressive language models, researchers increasingly use diffusion models, flow matching, energy-based methods, and consistency-style models across many frontier problems.

This course studies six major modern generative paradigms and systematically compares their strengths and limitations across three broad categories of data: token sequences, including language; continuous signals, such as images; and graph-based structures.

Why this course?

Large language models are the most visible success of modern generative AI, but they are not the whole story. Cutting-edge ML research increasingly studies generation for images, structured objects, scientific discovery, and hard optimization problems.

The course is designed for students who want to understand what lies beyond autoregressive LLMs: how different generative paradigms work, where they are being applied, and what current research questions remain open.

Who should take this course?

  • NLP and LLM students interested in diffusion, flow matching, and alternatives to autoregressive models.
  • ML students interested in modern generative modeling across multiple data types and applications.
  • CSD students interested in learning-based approaches to NP-hard problems and graph-structured generation.
  • OR/Economics students interested in machine-learning-based optimization, decision making, and structured generative methods.

Prerequisites

Students should have prior exposure to introductory machine learning, probability and statistics, and basic optimization. Familiarity with modern large language models is strongly recommended but not required. Prior experience with diffusion models, flow matching, graph generation, combinatorial optimization, or advanced deep learning systems is not assumed.

Course organization

Instructor-led lectures

The instructor will introduce each core generative paradigm through canonical examples.

  • Autoregressive models through language modeling
  • Energy-based models through image generation
  • Diffusion models through image generation
  • Flow matching through image generation
  • Consistency / IMM-style models through image generation
  • Autoregressive reinforcement learning for combinatorial optimization
  • Non-autoregressive reinforcement learning for combinatorial optimization
  • Diffusion models for combinatorial optimization

Student-led lectures

Student teams will present frontier research topics. Each topic takes one lecture, covers at least two generative paradigms, and is based on a curated paper set.

Object CategoryPossible Topics
Sequential TokensCoding, mathematical reasoning, diffusion language models
Continuous SignalsFast generation, controllable generation, diffusion vs. flow matching
Graph StructuresMIS, MaxCut, SAT, molecules, proteins, other NP-hard problems

Course format

  • No traditional homework.
  • No midterm or final exams.
  • Each lecture includes one to three checkpoint questions, taking approximately 10–15 minutes.
  • Students will submit answers to checkpoint questions through Gradescope. AI tools may not be used before submission.
  • Each student will give a student-led lecture. Teaming is possible, depending on the number of enrolled students.
